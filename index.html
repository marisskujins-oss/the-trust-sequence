01 The book

Most decisions don't stall on price. They stall on trust.

Whichever chair you are in, trust gets earned in the same order. This book shows you that order, so the yes you walk out with is one you can walk back in on.

Think about the last time you had to decide on something that mattered, offered by someone who knew more about it than you did. A builder, an agency, a software partner. You weren't short of information. You were short of a reason to believe it. And on the other side of the table, if they were any good, someone was working out how to earn that. This book is about what happens in that gap, from both chairs.

Patterns, not personality

Trust gets earned in a predictable order. Once you can see it, you stop running on gut feel, whether you're the one asking for the yes or the one giving it.

From real conversations

Every framework comes from recorded calls, not hypotheticals or case studies. Conversations where money changed hands and real decisions were made.

Ready for the next big yes

No philosophy chapters to wade through. Each framework tells you what to look for and what to ask for, the next time you sit across from someone who hasn't decided yet, or who is waiting on you to.

The mechanics don't change

Bikes sold online from a London flat and half-million-dollar digital projects: same instincts, same sequence, different zeros. A renovation or a new hire works the same way.

02 Frameworks

Named frameworks for specific moments

Every decision has moments where it moves forward or quietly dies. These frameworks name those moments, and what each side of the table should be doing when they arrive.

01 Framework

The Three Doors

Safety, then Context, then Decision. Skip one and the conversation breaks. If you're the one deciding, it doubles as a test: never say yes in a room where you couldn't say "I don't understand."

07 Framework

Cards on the Table

A seller you can trust tells you what you don't need and names the risk before you think of it. A buyer who knows this asks what the other side gains. Either way, the cards go face up.

11 Framework

The Soft Close

If you have to push for the signature, the moment has already passed. A good yes is built from small agreements along the way, and a good buyer notices when they're being hurried.

03 The author

Maris Skujins

I didn't go to business school. I'm educated as a rock, if we're being honest. What I did do is build a company from a London flat, restoring bikes and shipping them to 33 countries, then spend the next decade selling complex digital projects across 20+ countries, sitting in hundreds of rooms where someone had to decide whether to trust us with their money.

A few hundred recorded calls in, I stopped thinking about what worked and started asking why. Why did some conversations end in a yes when the product was the same, the price was the same, and the team was the same? The answer was always the same thing: the sequence in which trust was built. The best calls followed it. The worst ones skipped a step.

This book pulls that sequence apart and names the pieces. I sell for a living, so read it as what I would want you to know when you sit across from me.
