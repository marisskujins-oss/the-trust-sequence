About the book

The Trust Sequence: Walk Out With a Yes

A yes you can walk back in on. How people decide to trust you with their money, built from 600+ recorded conversations.

Why I wrote it

Somewhere past the five hundredth recorded call I stopped asking what had worked and started asking why. The product was the same, the price was the same and the team was the same, and one conversation ended with a signature while the one before it ended with "we'll think about it".

The answer was the order in which trust was built. In the calls that closed, the other person felt safe before they were asked anything, understood the decision before they saw a number, and knew what we stood to gain before they signed. In the calls that stalled, one of those steps was skipped, and no discount put it back.

I sell for a living, and most of what I have read about selling was written for the seller alone. This book is written for both chairs. If you need a yes, it shows you the order to earn it in. If you are the one who has to give it, it shows you what a trustworthy seller does at each step, and what it means when they do not.

The 13 frameworks

Each one is told through a real account and shown from both sides of the table.

01

The Three Doors.

Safety, then Context, then Decision, in that order.

If you are deciding: say yes only in a meeting where you could have said "I don't understand".

02

Lower the Walls.

People who feel safe tell you the truth, and the truth is what you are there for.

If you are deciding: say early what you do not know, and watch how they react.

03

The Power of Unknown.

Admitting what you do not know makes everything else you say believable.

If you are deciding: ask until you reach "I don't know". Someone who says yes to everything either does not know or is not telling you.

04

Narrative Before Numbers.

A number persuades only after the story has made it mean something.

If you are deciding: ask for it in plain language, and if they cannot give it, you cannot judge the offer.

05

Start at the End.

"What are we trying to achieve?" produces a decision; "what are we doing?" produces a task list.

If you are deciding: write down the outcome before you shop.

06

Authority Before Architecture.

The best solution fails when it is shown to the wrong person.

If you are deciding: bring in the people on your side who will live with it, and ask who on their side can commit.

07

Cards on the Table.

Say what you earn and what the other side does not need.

If you are deciding: ask what they gain from this and what in the offer you could leave out.

08

Phase It or Lose It.

The first step is the smallest one that proves something and can be undone, and it earns the right to propose the next.

If you are deciding: ask for the step you could walk away from.

09

Measure Twice.

In every complex deal, discovery changes the first number, and that is the process working.

If you are deciding: expect the number to move and ask what would move it and by how much.

10

Sell the Question.

The other side's uncertainty is the deal. Answering it can be paid work, and the answer belongs to them.

If you are deciding: you can buy an answer before you buy a solution.

11

The Soft Close.

A good yes is built from small agreements, so the signature is a formality.

If you are deciding: notice the moment you have already decided, and notice when you are being hurried.

12

The Orchestrated Team.

Who shows up, when and in what role is a trust signal.

If you are deciding: meet the people who will do the work before you sign.

13

The Paper Trail.

Spoken trust fades; written trust lasts. Numbered options, owners and dates.

If you are deciding: get it in writing, and write your own recap.

Where it comes from

600+ recorded conversations. 350+ deals. 20+ countries. Every account in the book is a real one, anonymised where the client asked. The stories are commercial, because that is where the evidence is. The principles work the same way for a renovation, a hire, a loan, or a budget you need your board to approve.
